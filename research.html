01
Understanding Abstract Concepts

How do learners represent abstract mathematical concepts?

Learners can represent the same mathematical concept in multiple ways. I use zero and negative numbers as model systems for understanding how mathematical representations emerge across development, how they vary across individuals and contexts, and how those representations shape problem solving.

Zero as a reference point

Children initially understand zero in terms of collections, where it represents absence. My work examines the development of a spatial conception of zero as a reference point between positive and negative numbers, and how this conceptual shift relates to integer reasoning.

Negative number representations

My research challenges the idea that learners converge on a single mature representation of negative numbers. Across developmental, experimental, and large-scale classroom studies, I find substantial variability in how learners represent negative magnitudes.

Representational dynamics

This work motivates a broader account of mathematical cognition in which representations are flexible, context-dependent, and dynamic. Rather than asking only which representation a learner possesses, I examine when different representations emerge and what determines their recruitment during problem solving.

02
Encoding External Representations

How do external representations shape mathematical reasoning?

Learners' reasoning depends not only on what they know, but also on which aspects of mathematical structure their environments make salient. I study how patterns, diagrams, and other external representations direct attention toward mathematical relationships and shape the strategies learners use.

Seeing structure in patterns

Pattern learning provides a window into how learners identify mathematical structure. My work examines how attention to repeating units and systematic change supports pattern extension, generalization, and broader mathematical knowledge.

Directing attention

Instructional supports do not influence learning uniformly. Across experimental studies, I have examined how gesture, imitation, verbal direction, and perceptual highlighting affect what children attend to and which strategies they recruit.

Diagrams and algebraic reasoning

I also study how diagrams make relational structure visible during algebraic problem solving. Work with intelligent tutoring systems examines how diagrammatic scaffolding and self-explanation can shape students' strategies, efficiency, and transfer.

03
Human–AI Interactions

How can AI provide new windows into mathematical reasoning and learning?

AI is both a new source of evidence about learners' mathematical thinking and an increasingly important part of the environments in which learning occurs. My current work examines both possibilities through AI-enabled measurement, learning analytics, and the study of human-AI interaction in authentic educational settings.

AI-enabled measurement

In collaboration with EdLight, I use longitudinal data from authentic, standards-aligned mathematics tasks to model students' developing knowledge. This work asks whether AI-enabled data can reveal patterns of mastery that are difficult to infer from individual responses or simple performance summaries.

Student-AI interaction

Through a research-practice-industry partnership involving the University of Florida, Miami-Dade County Public Schools, and Google, I study how high-school students interact with generative AI during mathematics learning.

Learning from interaction traces

I use learning analytics and LLM-assisted methods to characterize what students ask AI to do, the support AI provides, and how students engage with that support. This creates new opportunities to connect human-AI interaction with mathematical reasoning and learning over time.

Current Work
Human-AI interaction in high school mathematics. Examining how students use generative AI, how they engage with AI-generated mathematical support, and how patterns of interaction relate to learning.
Longitudinal measurement of mathematical mastery. Using AI-enabled student work and Bayesian Knowledge Tracing to estimate developing mathematical knowledge across authentic classroom tasks.
Looking Forward

Connecting minds, representations, and AI.

My current research brings these three strands together. I am particularly interested in how the external representations AI provides interact with the internal representations learners recruit, and in distinguishing AI support that improves immediate performance from interactions that produce durable changes in learners' own mathematical reasoning.
