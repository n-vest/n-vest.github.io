Research

How do people make sense of mathematics?

My research examines how learners build mathematical knowledge from internal and external representations, and how new technologies can reveal and support those processes.

01

Learning & Adapting Abstract Concepts

How do learners build and adapt representations of abstract mathematical concepts across development and contexts?

02

Encoding External Representations

How do external representations, including patterns in shapes and numbers and diagrams, help learners encode relationships in mathematics?

03

Human-AI Interactions

How can interactions with AI provide new windows into learners' mathematical reasoning and support productive learning?

About

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the School of Teaching and Learning at the University of Florida, where I study how people learn mathematics and how educational technologies can better support that learning.

My research examines the cognitive processes that shape numerical understanding, especially how learners build on existing knowledge to make sense of new mathematical concepts. I study these processes across development and in AI-mediated learning environments.

I earned my Ph.D. in Developmental Psychology at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, where I was a member of the Cognitive Development and Communication Lab.
